# Unit 3 Tutor Playbook: Music

Use this file to help an A2-B1 learner describe musical preferences, artists, achievements, talent shows, live events, and suggestions naturally.

## Knowledge map

Main ideas: music styles, artists, instruments, performance, achievement, online popularity, talent competitions, audience opinions, invitations, and event experiences.

Core contrasts:

- past event versus present achievement
- performer versus performance
- fact versus opinion about an artist
- suggestion versus response
- to-infinitive versus bare infinitive

## Vocabulary intelligence

Teach natural combinations:

- sing a song / write music / compose a song
- play the piano, drums, guitar, or trumpet
- perform live / give a performance
- attend a concert or festival
- release a single or album
- upload a cover-song video
- attract an audience
- receive or win an award
- become an online star
- enter a talent show
- audition for a competition
- reach the semi-final or final
- vote for a contestant
- be eliminated from the competition
- receive positive comments

Meaning distinctions:

- musician: someone who plays or creates music
- singer: someone who sings
- artist: a broad word for a creative performer or creator
- performance: the act or event; performer: the person
- audience: people watching or listening at an event
- fan: someone who strongly likes an artist or group
- single: one song released separately
- cover: a new performance of a song first recorded by someone else

## Grammar decision rules

Compound sentences:

- and adds a similar idea
- but introduces contrast
- or presents alternatives
- so introduces a result
- Join two complete clauses with a comma before the coordinating conjunction when appropriate.

Infinitive patterns:

- plan, decide, hope, want + to-infinitive
- make or let + person + bare infinitive
- can, should, must + bare infinitive
- Examples: "They decided to perform." "The song made me smile." "Her parents let her attend."

Past simple and present perfect:

- Past simple for a finished time: "She performed there last year."
- Present perfect for experience or achievement without a finished time: "She has won three awards."

Relative clause:

- who describes a person: "a singer who writes her own songs"
- which or that describes a thing: "a song that became popular"

## Common learner errors and concise corrections

- "He can plays guitar." -> "He can play the guitar."
- "She is a talent singer." -> "She is a talented singer."
- "They decided perform." -> "They decided to perform."
- "The song made me to feel happy." -> "The song made me feel happy."
- "I have seen the concert last night." -> "I saw the concert last night."
- "He has won an award in 2024." -> "He won an award in 2024."
- "There had many singers." -> "There were many singers."
- "I very like pop music." -> "I really like pop music."

## Pronunciation intelligence

Help the learner notice word families and stress shifts:

- 'music, mu'sician, 'musical
- 'performer versus per'form
- 'singer, 'programme, 'audience
- re'lax, at'tract, de'cide, re'ceive

Use tapping or underlining to mark the strongest syllable. Do not overcorrect accent when the word remains understandable.

## Conversation scenarios

1. Artist profile: describe skills, early career, achievements, and why the artist is admired.
2. Music-show invitation: make a suggestion, accept or decline, and arrange a time and place.
3. Talent-show judge: give one positive comment and one constructive suggestion.
4. Festival reporter: describe atmosphere, activities, performers, and feelings.
5. Friendly debate: compare live music with recorded music or online videos.

Useful follow-ups:

- What makes this artist talented?
- How did the artist become famous?
- Which achievement is most impressive?
- Shall we attend the show together?
- What was the atmosphere like?
- How did the performance make you feel?

## Reading and reasoning knowledge

A talent competition commonly includes auditions, selected participants, live rounds, judges' comments, audience voting, semi-finals, and a final. The singer with the strongest audience support may win, while the lowest-voted participant may be eliminated. Ask learners to separate the judges' role from the audience's role and explain how the show can help a career.

## Writing support

For a music-event blog:

1. identify the event, time, place, and companions
2. describe performers and atmosphere
3. report two or three activities
4. explain feelings and the most memorable moment
5. close with an overall evaluation or recommendation

Useful descriptive language: lively, exciting, relaxing, memorable, crowded, friendly, impressive, perform live, greatest hits, party atmosphere.

## Answer-quality rubric

- relevance: answers the question about music or an event
- range: uses accurate music vocabulary and descriptive language
- grammar: selects suitable tense, conjunction, or infinitive pattern
- development: supports preference with a reason and example

Feedback order: specific strength; one priority correction; improved version retaining the learner's opinion; one natural follow-up.

## Original practice bank

- Introduce an artist without naming them and let the tutor guess.
- Compare two music styles using and, but, or, and so.
- Explain how an unknown singer could become popular online.
- Role-play inviting a friend to a Saturday music show.
- Judge a fictional performance using two positive details and one suggestion.
- Write a short blog about an imagined festival using the past simple.

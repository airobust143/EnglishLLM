# Unit 1: Family Life

## Learning goals

Help a Grade 10 English learner discuss family roles, household chores, routines, and fair responsibility sharing. Practise the present simple for routines and the present continuous for actions happening now.

## Core context

Nam cannot play football because he is preparing dinner. His mother usually cooks, but she is working late today. His sister usually helps with cooking, but she is studying for her exams. Nam's family divides housework: his mother cooks and shops for groceries; his father cleans and does heavy lifting; his sister does the laundry; Nam does the washing-up and puts out the rubbish. Minh says his mother does the chores while his father is the breadwinner. Nam thinks sharing chores is fair.

## Useful language

- household chores, homemaker, breadwinner, groceries
- do the cooking, do the laundry, do the washing-up
- put out the rubbish, clean the house, do the heavy lifting
- divide the housework equally, help with the cooking
- usually, often, every day, today, right now

## Grammar focus

- Present simple for habits: "My mother usually does the cooking."
- Present continuous for an action now or a temporary situation: "She is working late today."
- Opinion frames: "I think children should..."; "In my opinion..."; "I strongly believe that..."

## Pronunciation focus

Practise initial consonant blends /br/, /kr/, and /tr/. Useful examples include breadwinner, breakfast, brown; crash, crane, cream; and track, tree, train. Help the learner keep both consonants audible instead of inserting a vowel between them.

## Expanded vocabulary and reading ideas

Teach the ideas of responsibility, gratitude, character, and family bonds. A useful reading theme is that sharing housework helps young people develop life skills, finish duties they may not enjoy, appreciate their parents' work, and feel part of a team. Treat these as discussion ideas rather than fixed opinions.

## Skills progression

- Reading: identify a writer's main claim and supporting benefits of doing chores.
- Speaking: give reasons why children should or should not help at home; respond politely to a different opinion.
- Listening: identify specific details about family support and weekly routines.
- Writing: describe two or three family routines in an informal email. Organise each routine by what the family does, how often it happens, and how it strengthens family bonds.
- Everyday English: distinguish strong opinions ("I strongly believe...") from neutral opinions ("In my opinion..." or "I suppose...").
- Culture: discuss how truthfulness, respect for older people, good manners, and family time can function as family values while recognising that values vary among families and cultures.

## Additional practice prompts

1. Compare what a family member usually does with what they are doing today.
2. Explain two benefits of sharing housework.
3. Politely agree or disagree with the idea that chores are only an adult's responsibility.
4. Write a short email describing a regular family meal, activity, or visit.

## Practice and review guidance

Ask learners about their real family routines. Encourage a reason after each opinion. Correct only the most important error, then give a natural improved sentence. Accept different family arrangements and avoid stereotypes about gender roles.

Reference answers from the source context: Nam's mother is not cooking now; everyone in Nam's family does some housework; the children in Minh's family do not do housework.

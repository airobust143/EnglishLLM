# Unit 1 Tutor Playbook: Family Life

Use this file as teaching guidance, not as a script to recite. Keep the learner near CEFR A2-B1 and prioritise useful spoken English.

## Knowledge map

Main ideas: household chores, family roles, fair division of work, routines, responsibility, gratitude, teamwork, and family bonds.

Core contrasts:

- routine versus action now
- personal opinion versus supporting reason
- traditional role versus shared responsibility
- a chore name versus the verb phrase used with it

## Vocabulary intelligence

Teach words in natural chunks:

- do housework / do household chores
- do the cooking / laundry / washing-up / heavy lifting
- clean the house / kitchen / floor
- shop for groceries
- put out the rubbish
- prepare dinner
- divide or share the housework equally
- help with a chore
- take responsibility for a task
- strengthen family bonds
- appreciate someone's hard work
- earn money / be the breadwinner

Meaning distinctions:

- chore: a regular small job at home
- housework: uncountable general work needed in a home
- homemaker: a person whose main work is managing the home
- breadwinner: a person who earns most of a family's income
- routine: an activity repeated regularly
- responsibility: a duty a person is expected to complete

Avoid "a housework" and "houseworks". Say "some housework", "a chore", or "household chores".

## Grammar decision rules

Present simple:

- Use for habits, routines, facts, and frequency.
- Common markers: usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, on Saturdays.
- Third-person singular needs -s or -es: "My father cleans the house."

Present continuous:

- Use for something happening now, around now, or temporarily.
- Form: am/is/are + verb-ing.
- Common markers: now, right now, at the moment, today, this week.

Contrast model: "Lan usually helps with the cooking, but she is studying today."

Should / shouldn't:

- Use to give an opinion or advice.
- Strong answer pattern: claim + reason + example.
- Example pattern: "Children should help with chores because they learn responsibility. For example, they can clean their rooms."

## Common learner errors and concise corrections

- "My mother cook every day." -> "My mother cooks every day."
- "I am do the washing-up now." -> "I am doing the washing-up now."
- "I usually am cleaning my room." -> "I usually clean my room."
- "She is work late today." -> "She is working late today."
- "I make the laundry." -> "I do the laundry."
- "My family share housework equal." -> "My family shares the housework equally."
- "Children should to help." -> "Children should help."

Correct one high-value error at a time. Preserve the learner's intended meaning.

## Conversation scenarios

1. Family interview: ask who cooks, shops, cleans, and does the laundry.
2. Busy evening: explain why one family member cannot do their usual chore today.
3. Fairness discussion: compare two ways families divide chores.
4. Advice role-play: one teenager dislikes chores; the other explains two benefits.
5. Routine planning: agree on a simple weekend chore schedule.

Useful follow-ups:

- How often do you do that?
- Who usually helps you?
- What are they doing today instead?
- Do you think that division is fair? Why?
- Which chore teaches the most useful life skill?

## Reading and reasoning knowledge

A learner should be able to explain that chores may develop practical life skills, responsibility, persistence, gratitude, character, and teamwork. Do not require agreement: accept a different position when it is explained clearly. Distinguish a main idea from examples such as laundry, cleaning, and caring for others.

## Writing support

For a short email about family routines, coach this structure:

1. friendly greeting and reason for writing
2. routine one: what, when, and why it matters
3. routine two: what, when, and how people feel
4. short closing question and sign-off

Useful connectors: first, also, every evening, at weekends, because, so, finally.

## Answer-quality rubric

Evaluate on four dimensions without giving numeric scores unless asked:

- relevance: directly answers the question
- language: understandable grammar and appropriate unit vocabulary
- development: includes a reason, example, or detail
- naturalness: sounds like real spoken or written English

Feedback order: name one specific strength; correct one important issue; offer an improved answer that retains the learner's idea; ask one short next-step question.

## Original practice bank

- What chore did you do yesterday, and how did it help your family?
- Compare your normal weekday routine with today.
- Which is more important for teenagers: more study time or sharing chores?
- Make a fair chore plan for four people.
- Describe a family routine you would like to start.
- Politely disagree with someone who says housework is only a parent's job.

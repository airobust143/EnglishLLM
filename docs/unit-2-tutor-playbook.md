# Unit 2 Tutor Playbook: Humans and the Environment

Use this file to guide an A2-B1 learner from simple green vocabulary to clear suggestions, plans, predictions, and cause-effect explanations.

## Knowledge map

Main ideas: green living, carbon footprints, waste, recycling, energy, environmental events, community awareness, plans, predictions, advice, and expected results.

Core contrasts:

- an existing plan versus a decision or prediction
- active versus passive focus
- environmental problem versus practical solution
- suggestion versus expected result

## Vocabulary intelligence

Teach natural combinations:

- adopt a greener lifestyle
- reduce your carbon footprint
- raise public awareness
- protect the environment
- conserve energy and natural resources
- switch off unused appliances
- reduce single-use plastic
- reuse bags and bottles
- recycle paper, glass, metal, and plastic
- pick up litter / put rubbish in a bin
- organise a clean-up activity
- plant trees and flowers
- set up a club / join a campaign
- collect used items for donation

Meaning distinctions:

- litter: waste left in a public place
- rubbish or waste: unwanted material in general
- recycle: process material so it can be used again
- reuse: use the same object again without processing it
- eco-friendly: causing little harm to the environment
- carbon footprint: greenhouse-gas emissions connected with a person, product, or activity

## Grammar decision rules

Will:

- decision made while speaking: "I forgot the lights. I'll switch them off."
- prediction based on belief: "I think more students will join."
- promise or offer: "I'll help with the clean-up."

Be going to:

- plan decided earlier: "We are going to plant trees on Saturday."
- prediction based on visible evidence: "Look at those clouds. It is going to rain."

Passive voice:

- Use when the action or result matters more than the actor.
- present: am/is/are + past participle
- past: was/were + past participle
- future: will be + past participle
- planned future: am/is/are going to be + past participle
- Example: "More bins will be placed near the school gate."

Advice and results:

- "You should..." / "You shouldn't..."
- "If I were you, I would..."
- "By doing this, we can..."
- "This will reduce / prevent / improve..."

## Common learner errors and concise corrections

- "We will going to clean the park." -> "We are going to clean the park."
- "I think it is going to help in the future." -> Use "will help" for a belief without present evidence.
- "More trees will planted." -> "More trees will be planted."
- "The club was set up from students." -> "The club was set up by students."
- "People should to recycle." -> "People should recycle."
- "This helps reduce the pollute." -> "This helps reduce pollution."
- "We must protect environment." -> "We must protect the environment."

## Conversation scenarios

1. Green club interview: explain the club's aims and next event.
2. Advice desk: suggest three ways to reduce a student's carbon footprint.
3. Event planning: assign clean-up, donation, and media team responsibilities.
4. School audit: identify an environmental problem and match it to a result-focused solution.
5. Town meeting: persuade people to support more trees, bins, or recycling points.

Useful follow-ups:

- What result do you expect from that action?
- Is this a plan or a prediction?
- Who will be responsible for it?
- How can students raise awareness?
- Which idea is easiest to start this week?

## Reading and reasoning knowledge

A green-living text may compare behaviours such as using many plastic bags, buying local or organic food, dropping litter, switching off appliances, carrying reusable items, and recycling. Train the learner to identify the writer's purpose, connect each action to an environmental effect, and choose a title representing the whole text.

## Writing support

For a suggestion paragraph:

1. topic sentence naming the place and goal
2. first suggestion
3. example of how to do it
4. expected result
5. second suggestion and result
6. conclusion showing that small actions matter

Useful connectors: first, second, for example, by doing this, as a result, in conclusion.

## Answer-quality rubric

- relevance: addresses the environmental question
- accuracy: correct future form, passive structure, or advice pattern
- cause and effect: links an action to a realistic result
- specificity: includes a concrete person, place, time, or method

Feedback order: specific strength; one correction; natural improved answer; one follow-up asking for a result or example.

## Original practice bank

- Make one spontaneous green decision with will and one prior plan with be going to.
- Explain how three school objects can be reused or recycled.
- Rewrite "Students collect bottles every Friday" in the passive voice.
- Design a one-day Go Green event with three teams.
- Give advice to a friend who uses many plastic bags.
- Write a six-sentence proposal to improve your classroom or neighbourhood.
